2. SIGN OF THE SCALING ERROR -- established from the solver, not from fit
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
pipeline.py:50 gives the solver's closed form:

    Q_new / Q0 = (1 + delta) ** (Es / (Es - Ed))

With a reduction of magnitude d (so delta = -d), Es > 0 and Ed < 0:

    k = Es / (Es - Ed)  in (0, 1)
    S(d) = Q0 * [1 - (1 - d)^k]          savings as a function of shock
    S'(d) = Q0 * k * (1 - d)^(k-1)

k - 1 < 0, so (1-d)^(k-1) INCREASES in d, so S' increases: S is CONVEX on
[0,1), and S(0) = 0. Convexity with S(0)=0 gives, for pi in (0,1):

    S(pi*d) <= pi * S(d)        hence      S(pi*d)/pi <= S(d)

My estimate divides the re-solved weighted savings by pi, i.e. forms
S(pi*d)/pi. It therefore UNDERSTATES the true unweighted S(d).

  PREDICTION: the re-solve lands ABOVE 54.226594, not below.

Magnitude, second order: pi*S(d) - S(pi*d) ~= Q0*k*(1-k)*d^2*pi*(1-pi)/2, so
the relative gap is ~ (1-k)*d*pi*(1-pi)/2. Global actual/naive = 0.479 gives
k ~= 0.48. Cross-checking against the code's own measured 0.14% at pi = 0.86
implies (1-k)*d ~= 0.023, which at pi(1) = 0.9948 scales to ~0.006%.

  PREDICTION: above 54.226594 by roughly 0.002 - 0.004 Mt,
              i.e. ~54.229 - 54.231, and in no case by as much as 0.01 Mt.

If it lands BELOW 54.226594, the convexity argument or the assumption that pi
enters only through the shock is wrong, and that is a finding about the model,
not a tolerance to widen.
